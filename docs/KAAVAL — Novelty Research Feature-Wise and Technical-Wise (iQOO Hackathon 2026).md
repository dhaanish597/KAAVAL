# KAAVAL — Novelty Research: Feature-Wise and Technical-Wise (iQOO Hackathon 2026)

## TL;DR
- **The core concept is genuinely novel and defensible**: no shipping product or hackathon project compares a *spoken* sales pitch against the *written* document for the BUYER and reports only a typed delta without a verdict. The closest prior art (VoiceClaim Auditor, Discharge Buddy, Pixel/Samsung Scam Detection) all either render verdicts/confidence scores, compare against a person's own instructions, or serve sellers — so KAAVAL's "delta not verdict" line is the single strongest, most defensible differentiator and should anchor the pitch.
- **Most regulatory/stat claims in the brief check out, but two need correction**: the "IRDAI 2026 insurance law amendments mandating need-analysis/signed benefit illustrations" claim is WRONG as stated — the Act that passed (Sabka Bima Sabki Raksha, assent 20 Dec 2025, in force 5 Feb 2026) is mostly about 100% FDI and gives IRDAI *powers* over commission disclosure, not the specific mandates; and the "RBI 2026 guidelines classifying mis-selling as an RBC violation" is unverified (RBI has only said it is *working on* guidelines).
- **Technically, the highest-risk item (on-device code-switched Tamil–English ASR) is real**: there is essentially no real Tamil–English code-switch speech corpus and reported WERs are in the 56–68% range. The realistic 30-hour path is number/keyword spotting + a financial-term bias list on top of AI4Bharat IndicConformer Tamil (CTC ONNX via sherpa-onnx) plus the English SpeechRecognizer pass — NOT full dual-language transcription. MediaPipe LLM Inference is now deprecated in favour of LiteRT-LM; plan Tier-2 accordingly.

## Key Findings

### 1. Novelty verdict on the core concept
The "on-device witness that compares spoken promises to written clauses, for the buyer, and reports only a typed delta" is **novel as an integrated product**. The novelty is not in any single component (ASR, OCR, claim extraction all exist) but in the **combination + the load-bearing constraint** ("it never accuses, it quotes"). The strongest threats are conceptual adjacency, not direct competition. Name them explicitly in the pitch and pre-empt.

**Prior-art threats, ranked by closeness:**
1. **VoiceClaim Auditor** (lablab.ai hackathon app) — the closest functional twin. It "listens to live conversations, detects factual claims, breaks them into atomic assertions, and verifies them in real time using Speechmatics, Bright Data, and AI/ML API with **evidence-backed verdicts and confidence scores**." Three decisive differences: (a) it renders verdicts and confidence scores — exactly what KAAVAL refuses to do; (b) it verifies claims against the open web/external sources, not against the specific document in the buyer's hand; (c) it is cloud-dependent (Speechmatics, Bright Data are cloud APIs). KAAVAL's "delta between two values the user already possesses, fully offline, no score" is the clean wedge.
2. **Pixel Scam Detection / Samsung Galaxy (S26) Scam Detection** — on-device (Gemini Nano), real-time, analyses "conversation patterns commonly associated with scams," gives audio+haptic+visual alerts. This is the most powerful *conceptual* comparison because it is on-device and privacy-preserving too. But it is a **trained scam classifier that renders a verdict** ("this is likely a scam"), it is English-only, and it works on the caller's speech alone with no document cross-reference. Google's own support docs state it is "based on the corpus of knowledge about how scammers scam victims." KAAVAL has *no* scam training and renders *no* verdict — this is the "delta not verdict" distinction in its sharpest form. Note the pace: Scam Detection is already expanding from Pixel to the Samsung Galaxy S26 series and "more manufacturers."
3. **Discharge Buddy** (lablab.ai) — compares what a patient says against *their own* discharge instructions. Structurally similar (speech vs document) but it is self-comparison (cooperative, single party) not a spoken-claim-vs-written-clause check between two parties, and it is a healthcare-adherence tool.
4. **Seller-side conversational intelligence** (Gong, Chorus/ZoomInfo, Avoma, Nimitai, Mihup.ai) — all record and analyse sales conversations *for the seller*. This validates the pitch line "every one of them works for the person doing the selling." KAAVAL is buyer-side; this white space is real.
5. **Indian consumer insurance tools** (Insurance Samadhan, Polifyx, Beshak, Ditto, Bima Bharosa portal, Bima Sugam) — all are *post-hoc* grievance/redressal or comparison tools that act *after* the sale. None operate at the point of signing comparing live speech to the document. Insurance Samadhan explicitly positions itself as "gather all evidence… raise a complaint… escalate to the Insurance Ombudsman or IRDAI" — i.e., after the fact.

**How defensible is the "buyer-side white space" claim?** Strongly defensible for the specific mechanism (live spoken-vs-written delta, on-device, no verdict). Moderately exposed at the concept level because on-device scam detection is a fast-moving space (Pixel → Samsung → "expanding to more manufacturers"). **Pre-empt in the pitch**: "Scam detectors render a verdict from a scam-pattern model. We refuse to. We only quote the document back to you next to what you just heard. That means we work even when everyone in the room is honest — because the value is information parity, not accusation."

### 3. Ranked novel FEATURE ideas
(Each: what it is · novelty · rubric served · 30-hr effort · risk · constraint flag)

**Tier A — build in the window (highest score-per-hour):**

1. **Neutral follow-up question generator** ("Ask him: where in the document does it say 'guaranteed'?"). Turns a detected UNSUPPORTED/CONTRADICTED delta into a question the buyer can voice. *Novelty:* not seen in any prior art; a natural extension of "quote, don't accuse." *Rubric:* Novelty/Impact, Demo. *Effort:* Low (template per claim type). *Risk:* wording must stay interrogative, not accusatory. *Constraint:* SAFE — it asks, never concludes.
2. **Tamper-evident, device-attested claim ledger** (hash-chain each claim entry; sign the exported grievance packet with an Android Keystore/StrongBox-attested key; embed a trusted timestamp). *Novelty:* strong — makes the packet credible for an Ombudsman filing; no consumer tool does hardware-attested evidence. *Rubric:* Technical depth, Office Kit, Novelty. *Effort:* Medium (Keystore key + hash chain is doable; full StrongBox attestation cert chain is stretch). *Risk:* over-scoping. *Constraint:* SAFE — it authenticates a record, does not judge it.
3. **Grievance-packet mapping to real IRDAI channels** (Bima Bharosa portal, Insurance Ombudsman jurisdiction, 30-day free-look clock). *Novelty:* medium; the mapping to specific 2024/2025 channels is fresh and concrete. *Rubric:* Office Kit, Impact. *Effort:* Low–Medium (static routing table + PDF section). *Constraint:* SAFE — cites process, renders no verdict.
4. **Free-look countdown tracker** (after signing, a 30-day timer with the exact CIS clauses the buyer flagged). *Novelty:* medium; ties directly to the verified 30-day rule. *Rubric:* Impact, Productivity fallback. *Effort:* Low. *Constraint:* SAFE.

**Tier B — mention in pitch / partial:**

5. **Discreet haptic alert pattern** (buyer glances at phone; a specific vibration = "a delta was logged"). *Novelty:* Pixel/Samsung already use haptics for scam alerts, so haptics per se are NOT novel — the novelty is a *content-neutral* pattern that signals "check your screen" without displaying a verdict. *Rubric:* Creative Phone Use. *Effort:* Low. *Constraint:* SAFE if the buzz means "look," not "danger."
6. **Disclosure modes (private vs disclosed evidence mode)** — private warning to the buyer vs an openly-shown "I'm keeping a record" mode. *Novelty:* the ethics framing is novel and demo-worthy. *Rubric:* Novelty/Impact. *Effort:* Low (UI toggle). *Risk:* one-party-consent law makes both legal, but disclosed mode is the ethical high ground — say so.
7. **Family/trusted-person handoff** (send the claim ledger, not the audio, to a relative). *Novelty:* medium. *Rubric:* Impact. *Effort:* Medium. *Constraint:* SAFE (claims only, DPDP-aligned).

**Tier C — Grand Finale roadmap (mention only):**

8. **Generalisation to other "spoken promise vs written contract" domains**: rental agreements, job offers (CTC vs offer letter), loans with bundled products, medical consent, used-vehicle sales. *Novelty:* broadens TAM; the taxonomy generalises. *Rubric:* Impact/vision. *Effort:* High. *Constraint:* SAFE — same delta engine.

**Ideas that would VIOLATE the constraint (do NOT build):** any "risk score," "mis-selling likelihood," colour-coded traffic-light gauge, or "this agent seems dishonest" flag. The brief's "Carbon Copy" UI thesis is correct: a colour ramp is a risk score drawn rather than computed.

### 4. Ranked novel TECHNICAL approaches

1. **Number/keyword spotting + financial-term bias list instead of full code-switched transcription.** Given there is essentially no real Tamil–English code-switch speech corpus and published code-switch WERs run 56–68% (Shah et al., arXiv:2006.05257), attempting full dual-language transcription in 30 hours is the biggest risk. The literature corroborates the difficulty: the HiACC corpus study (NIH PMC12329218) reports "ASR systems experience a 30–50% increase in Word Error Rate when transcribing code-switched speech compared to monolingual speech," and measured English–isiXhosa code-switch WER of 62.8–76.5% (arXiv:1807.10949) — reinforcing that no usable Tamil–English CS corpus exists. Instead, detect the *slots that matter* — numbers ("8%", "one year", "lakh"), and a curated bias/keyword list of financial terms ("guaranteed," "surrender," "lock-in," "FD," "return") in both Tamil and romanised forms. *Novelty:* genuine and defensible framing ("we don't transcribe the conversation, we spot the six claim types"). *Rubric:* Technical depth, Novelty. *Effort:* Medium. This is the recommended primary path.
2. **AI4Bharat IndicConformer Tamil (120M, hybrid CTC-RNNT, MIT) via CTC-ONNX in sherpa-onnx**, paired with Android SpeechRecognizer for the English pass. Confirmed: the CTC branch exports to ONNX (community weights exist, ~493 MB) and runs on Android CPU; the RNNT/transducer export to sherpa-onnx is currently broken (KeyError: 'dir' in the AI4Bharat NeMo fork), so a streaming transducer is not a 30-hour option. *Rubric:* Technical depth. *Effort:* Medium–High. *Risk:* offline (non-streaming) CTC means chunked, not truly live — design the demo around short utterances.
3. **Deterministic Tier-1 slot normalisation across Tamil/English** — spoken-number and time-span normalisation ("எட்டு சதவீதம்"→8%, "one year கழிச்சு"→lock-in=1yr, lakh/crore scaling), unit/tense normalisation, hedged-vs-asserted detection ("guaranteed" vs "expected/illustrative"). *Novelty:* this cross-lingual numeric normalisation layer is the real technical depth and is fully unit-testable in the pure-JVM :domain module. *Rubric:* Technical depth. *Effort:* Medium. *Constraint:* SAFE.
4. **Slot-level comparison first, small quantized NLI as stretch.** Represent CONSISTENT / UNSUPPORTED / CONTRADICTED as typed set-membership + value comparison, not as an entailment probability (which would imply a score). If enrichment is wanted, DeBERTa-v3-small MNLI (44M backbone / ~143M with vocab; MNLI ~88%) has an ONNX build (Xenova/nli-deberta-v3-small) that fits a mobile budget. *Rubric:* Technical depth. *Effort:* High. *Risk:* NLI outputs a probability — keep it OUT of the user-facing layer to avoid implying severity. *Constraint:* handle with care.
5. **Tier-2 enrichment with Gemma 3n E2B int4 — but plan for LiteRT-LM, not MediaPipe.** The MediaPipe LLM Inference API is now officially deprecated / maintenance-only; Google recommends migrating to LiteRT-LM. Gemma 3n E2B runs in ~2GB (E4B ~3GB); on a Snapdragon 8 Gen 3 (S24 Ultra) E4B decodes ~9 tok/s (GPU prefill ~548 tok/s). No official 8 Elite Gen 5 numbers exist yet. Constrain Tier-2 output with grammar/JSON schema (llama.cpp GBNF / XGrammar-style) so it can only emit valid claim JSON. *Rubric:* Technical depth. *Effort:* Medium (as best-effort, cuttable).
6. **Gemma 3n audio is NOT a safe Tamil ASR bet.** Gemma 3n's USM-based audio encoder is officially validated only for English↔Spanish/French/Italian/Portuguese (Google's Gemma 3n developer guide: "particularly strong AST results for translation between English and Spanish, French, Italian, and Portuguese"); Tamil audio ASR is undocumented and code-switched Tamil–English is entirely unvalidated. It has a 30-second clip cap and no streaming. Do not rely on it for the ASR path; if used at all, use it for post-hoc enrichment of already-transcribed text.
7. **OCR: ML Kit Text Recognition v2 (Latin) is correct for English documents.** Confirmed: v2 supports Latin, Chinese, Devanagari, Japanese, Korean — **no Tamil script**, so the brief's decision to keep documents English is sound. Devanagari support exists (for Hindi docs) as a future note. For layout-aware benefit-illustration tables, a small VLM (Gemma 3n vision) is a stretch enrichment; ML Kit's block/line/element structure covers the demo.
8. **Privacy as a visible technical feature.** Airplane mode on-stage; omit the INTERNET permission from the manifest entirely so judges can verify no network is even possible; audio discarded by default, only claims persist. This is DPDP-Rules-2025-aligned data minimisation and is itself a demonstrable novelty. *Rubric:* Novelty/Impact, Technical depth. *Effort:* Low. *Constraint:* SAFE.

### 5. Platform specifics (iQOO/vivo)
- **Office Kit** = vivo/iQOO OriginOS 6 phone-PC/Mac collaboration suite: screen mirroring, file transfer (Free Transfer / File EasyShare — no size/format limit), remote PC control, task handoff, and DocMaster (PDF conversion/annotation/signature). Requires OriginOS 6. This is the exact mechanism for the "advocate tier" file handoff and PDF grievance packet — the Green Light tier maps cleanly to Office Kit's file transfer + DocMaster. Confirmed available on iQOO 15 (OriginOS 6 / Android 16).
- **Snapdragon 8 Elite Gen 5 Hexagon NPU**: 37% faster than prior gen; supports INT2/INT4/INT8/INT16/FP8/FP16, micro-tile execution, **concurrency** (multiple models across NPU), and model encryption. LiteRT QNN accelerator benchmarks show up to 100× CPU / 10× GPU speedups; 56+ models run <5ms on the NPU. This supports running deterministic extraction + a small model concurrently. Thermal behaviour over a sustained 30–40 min session is unverified — test it; keep Tier-2 cuttable.
- **OriginOS 6 background restrictions**: aggressive battery optimisation could kill a long-running foreground mic/camera service — request foreground-service exemption and test on the actual device early.

## Recommendations

**Do in the 30-hour window (Red Light — phone must work standalone):**
1. Tier-1 deterministic slot extraction + cross-lingual number/time normalisation in the pure-JVM :domain module (fully unit-tested). This is your technical-depth spine and is demo-deterministic.
2. Number/keyword spotting with a financial-term bias list (Tamil + romanised) over English SpeechRecognizer + IndicConformer Tamil CTC-ONNX. Do NOT promise full code-switched transcription.
3. ML Kit Text Recognition v2 (Latin) OCR of an English benefit illustration; map the six claim types to CIS/benefit-illustration fixed fields.
4. The one-line Tamil delta alert (the "Carbon Copy" no-colour UI), the neutral follow-up question generator, and the discreet haptic "look at screen" pattern.
5. Visible privacy: airplane mode + no INTERNET permission in the manifest.
6. Office Kit handoff of a signed, hash-chained grievance-packet PDF (Green Light, strictly additive).

**Mention in the pitch only (built as thin stubs or slides):**

- Free-look 30-day countdown; IRDAI channel routing (Bima Bharosa / Ombudsman).
- Small on-device NLI for CONTRADICTED detection (keep the probability internal).

**Grand Finale roadmap:**

**Benchmarks/thresholds that change the plan:**
